## The Three Pillars

Every cold email campaign that works has all three of these. Miss one and the whole thing fails.

1. **Deliverability** — Your email must land in the primary inbox. Not promotions. Not spam. Primary.
2. **List quality** — You must be emailing the right person at the right company at the right time.
3. **Messaging value** — Your email must offer something the recipient actually wants.

If your emails aren't getting replies, diagnose in this order: deliverability first, list second, messaging last. Most people obsess over copy when their emails are landing in spam.

## Email Structure Rules

### Length
- **Optimal:** 70-90 words
- **Maximum:** 100 words. Never exceed this.
- Count the words. If you're over 100, cut. Every word must earn its place.

### Opening Line
- **Never** start with "I" — not "I noticed," not "I saw," not "I wanted to reach out"
- **Never** use "I hope this finds you well" or any variant
- **Never** use "I noticed that..." — this is the #1 tell that an email is AI-generated
- **Do** start with the prospect — their company, their situation, their problem
- **Do** reference something specific to their business in the first sentence

### Closing
- **Always** end with a question, never a hard CTA
- Bad: "Book a call here: [link]"
- Bad: "Let me know if you'd like to chat"
- Good: "Would it help if I sent over the full breakdown?"
- Good: "Is this something you're actively trying to solve?"
- The question should be low-commitment and easy to say yes to

### Formatting
- **No signatures** — the sending platform (SmartLead, Instantly, etc.) handles signatures. Never include "Best, [Name]" or any sign-off in the email script.
- **No "Subject:" lines** — subject lines are configured separately in the sending platform. Never include them in the email body.
- Plain text only. No HTML. No bold. No bullet points. No images.
- Short paragraphs — 1-2 sentences each. White space is your friend.

## The "So What?" Principle

This is the single most important framework for cold email copy.

Take your value proposition and keep asking "so what?" until you hit the emotional core.

**Example:**
- "We automate your outbound" — So what?
- "You'll send more emails" — So what?
- "You'll book more meetings" — So what?
- "You'll hit your revenue target without hiring 3 more SDRs" — **There it is.**

The last answer is what goes in the email. Not the feature. Not the benefit. The outcome that makes someone's life materially better.

**Another example:**
- "We build websites for contractors" — So what?
- "Your website will look better" — So what?
- "You'll get more leads from Google" — So what?
- "You'll stop paying $2,000/mo to HomeAdvisor for shared leads and own your pipeline instead" — **That's the one.**

## PS Lines — The Hidden Weapon

Adding a PS line increases response rates by up to 5x. Here's why: it introduces a second angle. If the main email doesn't resonate, the PS might.

**Rules for PS lines:**
- Must be a completely different value proposition than the main email
- Should feel casual and almost like an afterthought
- Under 20 words
- Can include social proof, a specific result, or an alternative hook

**Examples:**
- "PS — We did this for [similar company] last month. They booked 14 meetings in the first 2 weeks."
- "PS — I also put together a teardown of your current landing page if you want it. Free either way."
- "PS — Saw you're hiring for an SDR. This might make that unnecessary."

## Subject Lines

The subject line is not standalone. The prospect sees **subject + preview text + first line** as one unit in their inbox. Optimize for that full string.

### Rules
- Include the prospect's **first name** — it increases open rates significantly
- Use **mismatched capitalization** — it looks human, not marketing
  - Good: "quick question Alex"
  - Good: "alex - saw this"
  - Bad: "Quick Question For You, Alex"
- Keep it 3-6 words
- No clickbait. No emojis. No ALL CAPS.

### Examples
- "{{first_name}} - quick thought"
- "idea for {{company_name}}"
- "{{first_name}}, saw this and thought of you"
- "question about {{company_name}}"
- "{{first_name}} - 2 min"

## The Creative Ideas Campaign

Instead of pitching your product, show 3 specific tailored ideas for THEIR business. This is the highest-performing campaign format.

### How it works
1. Research the prospect's business (website, LinkedIn, reviews, competitors)
2. Come up with 3 specific, actionable ideas they could implement
3. Send the ideas for free in the email
4. The CTA is offering to help implement them

### Why it works
- It demonstrates competence before asking for anything
- It's impossible to mass-send (which makes it feel valuable)
- It uses **constrained creativity** — the constraint is their specific business, which forces genuine thinking

### Example structure
```
[Specific observation about their business]

I put together 3 ideas for {{company_name}} that could [specific outcome]:

1. [Specific idea referencing their actual product/service/market]
2. [Specific idea referencing something on their website or in their reviews]
3. [Specific idea referencing a competitor gap or market opportunity]

Happy to walk through how we've implemented #2 for [similar company type] — would a quick breakdown help?
```

## Personalization Rules

### What works
- Reference a specific industry pain point (not "I know marketing is hard" — instead "I know roofing companies in Phoenix are getting crushed by HomeAdvisor's new pricing")
- Reference something specific from their website, a review, a LinkedIn post, or a news article
- Reference a hiring signal, funding round, or product launch
- Each email must be unique per contact — not a template with merge fields swapped in

### What kills replies
- Generic AI personalization hurts reply rates by 143% compared to no personalization at all
- "I noticed your company is in the [industry] space" — this is worse than nothing
- "I saw on LinkedIn that you..." followed by something anyone could find in 2 seconds
- Compliments that feel automated: "Love what you're doing at [company]!"

### The standard
The email should read like someone spent 5 minutes researching the prospect and wrote a thoughtful, specific message. That's the bar. If it reads like a template, it fails.

## S-Tier Email Formula

The highest-performing format: **personalized email + genuine lead magnet**.

Give free value before asking for anything. The lead magnet must be:
- Actually useful (not a generic PDF)
- Specific to their business (not a one-size-fits-all)
- Delivered upfront (not gated behind a call)

**Examples of genuine lead magnets:**
- A custom landing page mockup for their business
- A teardown of their current funnel with specific fixes
- A competitive analysis of their top 3 competitors
- A list of 10 prospects they should be targeting (if you're selling lead gen)
- A script they can use immediately for their next campaign

## What to Avoid — The Blacklist

Never use these in any cold email:
- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "I wanted to reach out because..."
- "I'm [Name] from [Company]" as an opener
- "We help companies like yours..."
- "Would you be the right person to speak with?"
- "I'd love to set up a quick 15-minute call"
- "Let me know if you'd like to learn more"
- "Best regards" / "Cheers" / "Thanks" / any sign-off
- Bullet points or formatted lists in the email body
- Links in the first email (hurts deliverability)
- Attachments (hurts deliverability)
- Images or HTML formatting

## Prompt Template for Generating Cold Emails with Claude API

Use this system prompt when calling the Anthropic API to generate cold email scripts:

```
You are a cold email copywriter. Write a single cold email following these rules exactly:

STRUCTURE:
- 70-90 words. Never exceed 100.
- Never start with "I"
- Never use "I hope this finds you well" or "I noticed"
- End with a low-commitment question, not a hard CTA
- No signature, no sign-off, no "Best," no "Thanks"
- No "Subject:" line
- Plain text only. Short paragraphs (1-2 sentences each).

PERSONALIZATION:
- Reference something specific about the prospect's business
- Use the "So What?" principle — lead with the emotional outcome, not the feature
- The email must read like a human spent 5 minutes researching this prospect
- Never use generic statements that could apply to any company

TONE:
- Conversational, not formal
- Peer-to-peer, not salesperson-to-buyer
- Confident but not pushy
- Like a smart friend giving advice, not a vendor pitching

INPUT: You will receive prospect data including company name, contact name, industry, and any available context. Use all of it.
OUTPUT: Return ONLY the email body. No subject line. No signature. No explanation.
```
