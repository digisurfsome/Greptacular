## 1. Business Model Overview

### What This Is

A recurring-revenue agency that sells AI receptionist services (and bolt-on services) to local and online businesses. The core product is an AI voice agent that answers calls 24/7, sounds human, books appointments, and handles inquiries — all without a human employee.

### Why Recurring Revenue Beats Everything Else

| Model | Consistency | Scalability | Risk |
|-------|-------------|-------------|------|
| Drop shipping | Low (volatile months) | Medium | High |
| Amazon FBA | Medium | Medium | High |
| Trading | Very low | Low | Very high |
| AI Agency (recurring) | **High** | **Very high** | **Low** |

The value of this model is not any single sale. It is waking up every morning knowing a fixed dollar amount is hitting your account regardless of what you do that day. A client paying $500/month is worth $6,000/year minimum — and much more with upsells and retention.

### The Three Core Jobs of This Business

Every task you do maps to one of these three. If a task does not map to one of them, it is probably a distraction.

```
1. GET BOOKED CALLS      → Turn strangers into scheduled conversations
2. SELL THE SERVICE      → Convert calls to paying clients
3. FULFILL & RETAIN      → Deliver value, lock in monthly recurring
```

### Revenue Model Summary

- Primary product: AI receptionist = **$497–$500/month**
- Bolt-on (optional upsell or freebie): Google Reviews + SEO + Website = $97–$0/month added
- Goal: $10,000+/month recurring
- Path: 20–25 clients at $500 = $10,000–$12,500 MRR

## 2. Niche Selection Framework

### The Three Categories

| Category | Examples | Entry Difficulty | Saturation |
|----------|----------|-----------------|------------|
| Home Services | Roofers, HVAC, plumbers, movers, cleaners, electricians | Low | **Very low — blue ocean** |
| Professional Services | Law firms, accounting, mortgage brokers, real estate agents | Medium | Medium |
| High-Ticket Online | Coaches, info products, agencies, marketing firms | High | High |

### Blue Ocean Reasoning (Why Home Services Win for Beginners)

Home service businesses — the plumbers, roofers, HVAC techs, movers — are a blue ocean for one specific reason: **they do not believe AI can help them yet.**

This sounds like a negative. It is actually a massive advantage.

- No one is selling them AI → zero competition
- They have not been pitched before → no fatigue, no skepticism from oversaturation
- They have real problems AI solves → missed calls, after-hours demand, no-shows
- The sales job is: first sell them on AI itself, THEN sell your product

Professional services (real estate, law, mortgage) already know AI, already use AI, and already have AI receptionists. To win there, you have to compete on the quality of your agency and your personal brand — not on novelty.

### Niche Selection Decision Tree

```
Do you already work in an industry (real estate, construction, healthcare, etc.)?
  └── YES → Start with that industry. You know the language and pain points.
  └── NO  → Start with Home Services (broadest, least saturated, easiest pitch)
              └── Cast wide net across all home services
              └── After 10 clients, check: which type has 70%+ of clients?
                  └── That's your niche. Double down. Niche-specific ads and case studies.
```

### Niche Narrowing Strategy

- Start: "We serve all home service businesses"
- Month 2–3 check: If 7 out of 10 clients are roofers → you're a roofing AI agency now
- Power move: Run ads that say "Roofers in [City], we helped [Roofer A] and [Roofer B] get 40 more booked jobs per month"
- Result: Roofers call you because you clearly understand their world

This is not a trick question. The math is so lopsided that rational people say yes. Your job is to build to this question, not jump to it.

### SCRIPT 1 — Facebook Group Post

**Where used:** Business owner Facebook groups (local city groups, industry groups). Post directly in the group feed. No DM required at this stage — the goal is to get them to call the AI number.

```
Hey [City] business owners —

Quick question: do you want to be the ONLY business in your area 
taking calls after 5 PM?

When your competition closes, you keep getting 100% of the market demand.

I have an AI receptionist that:
✓ Takes calls 24/7
✓ Never misses a call
✓ Sounds like a real human
✓ Books people directly onto your calendar

Don't believe me? Call it yourself: [YOUR AI RECEPTIONIST NUMBER]

Throw it a curveball. You'll be blown away.

— [Your name], [Agency name]
```

**Notes:**
- Replace `[YOUR AI RECEPTIONIST NUMBER]` with your actual demo AI line
- The AI itself will handle the sale after they call — don't oversell in the post
- Post in 20+ groups per day for volume (this is the VA's job at scale)
- Vary the opener slightly so it doesn't get flagged as spam across groups

### SCRIPT 2 — Cold Call Script

**Where used:** Outbound calls to businesses you've identified as prospects (no website, on page 10 of Google, etc.). Low-friction — you are NOT selling on this call.

```
"Hey, is this [Owner Name / Business Name]?

Hey [Name], my name is [Your Name] — I run a local AI agency 
and I help [home service / professional] businesses capture 
the calls they're currently missing after hours.

I'm not going to pitch you anything right now — I just want 
to text you a number real quick.

It's my AI receptionist. Same one I set up for businesses like yours.

Just call it tonight when you have 2 minutes — maybe after dinner.

I think it'll genuinely blow you away.

Can I just shoot that number over to you real quick?

[Pause — they say yes]

Awesome. Sending it now. It's [AI number]. 

Just throw it a curveball. Ask it something weird. 
See how it handles it. We'll talk after if you're curious.

Thanks [Name], have a great day."
```

**Notes:**
- You are not selling on this call. You are selling the call-to-AI.
- "Throw it a curveball" is the key phrase — it invites curiosity and removes pressure
- Follow up the next day: "Hey, did you get a chance to call that number?"

### SCRIPT 4 — Phone Pitch / ROI Walkthrough (Dentist Framework)

**Where used:** On the booked call, after brief rapport. This is the main sales conversation. Adapt the business type and numbers to your niche.

```
"So tell me — what time does your [business] close each day?"

[They answer, e.g., "5 PM"]

"Got it. And when do you think most of your customers 
actually have time to call you?"

[They answer, e.g., "Maybe at lunch"]

"Right — and why at lunch specifically?"

[Let them reason through it]

"Exactly. Because if someone's calling a [dentist/plumber/HVAC], 
they either have money or insurance. And if they have money or insurance, 
they probably have a job. And if they have a job, 
they can't call during work hours.

So — here's the question I want you to think about for a second.

After 5 PM, when they're actually off work and finally have time to call —
what happens to those calls right now?"

[They say: "They go to voicemail" or "We miss them"]

"Right. Now, hypothetically — if you could keep your phone lines 
open 24/7, after hours, nights, weekends — 
how many extra calls do you think you'd get per day?"

[They answer — use their number, not yours]

"Okay so let's say [their number] calls a day.
That's [X × 30] extra calls per month.

Of those, how many typically become actual [patients/customers]?"

[They answer — use their percentage]

"So that's [X new customers] per month.
And what's the minimum someone spends with you? 
Not the expensive stuff — just the basic visit."

[They give you a number, e.g., $100]

"Okay, so [X customers] at [$100 minimum] — 
that's [$X] in added revenue per month.

Here's my question for you:
Would you pay $500 a month to make an extra [$X] every month?"

[Pause. Let them answer.]
```

**Notes:**
- Every number in this pitch comes from the owner, not from you. This is critical.
- When they say the numbers, they own the conclusion. You just do the math.
- The question "Would you pay $500 to make $X?" should land like a no-brainer, not a pitch.

## 6. Demo Call Strategy

### The Two-Step Demo Funnel

**Step 1 — Push them to call the AI**
All outreach (posts, cold calls, ads) leads here. The message is always some version of: "Don't take my word for it. Call the AI."

**Step 2 — AI books the calendar**
The AI receptionist takes the call, runs its script (see Script 3), handles curveballs, and pushes toward one outcome: "Let me book you in with [Your Name] for a quick 15-minute call."

### What the Demo AI Should Be Capable Of

- Introduce itself as an AI (and own it — transparency builds trust)
- Handle the "you're just an AI" objection (see Script 3)
- Answer questions about pricing, services, how it works
- Counter-pitch: explain why an AI that's good at sales is exactly what their business needs
- Collect name, business name, and availability
- Drop appointment onto your calendar (GoHighLevel or similar)

### Demo Scenarios to Build Out

Build at least three demo scenarios in your AI so it can handle different types of callers:

| Scenario | Caller Type | AI Behavior |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| After-hours call | Business owner calling late | Captures lead, explains it would handle this for their business |
| During-hours skeptic | Owner who thinks AI sounds robotic | Engages conversation, handles curveball questions |
| Job inquiry call | Someone looking for work | Handles gracefully, pivots to "I can have the owner follow up" |

## 7. Retention Strategy

### The Foundation Principle

The goal is not to have clients who *want* to keep paying you. The goal is to have clients who *need* to keep paying you.

```
[BUSINESS OWNER]
        │
        │ relies on
        ▼
[YOUR SERVICES]
   ├── AI Receptionist  (answers their phones 24/7)
   ├── Website          (you built it, you host it, it's yours)
   ├── Google Reviews   (your system controls review flow)
   └── SEO Optimization (your setup drives their search ranking)
```

If they stop paying you → they lose their phone answering system, their website, their review flow, and their SEO. Their business collapses.

They don't pay you because they want to. They pay you because if they didn't, they'd have to rebuild their entire digital infrastructure from scratch.

### Retention Tactics

1. **Bolt-ons as anchors** — Even free bolt-ons (website, reviews) create dependency. The more services you control, the harder it is to leave.

2. **Weekly VA check-ins** — The VA calls clients once a week to check in, report results, and reinforce the relationship. A client who hears from you regularly is a client who stays.

3. **Report results, not just effort** — Show them: "You got 47 new calls after hours this month. 12 booked appointments. Here's your Google review count vs. last month." Numbers make the value visible.

4. **The "you're winning" psychology** — When clients feel like they're getting more than they paid for, they stay. The free bolt-on script (Script 7) engineers this feeling deliberately.

## 8. Scaling Pyramid

### The Three Layers

```
              [YOU]
             /     \
      [VA Manager] [Sales Manager]
       /    |    \      |    \
     VA1   VA2   VA3  Closer1  Closer2
```

### Layer 1 — You + One VA ($400/month)

- VA works 8 hours/day
- Morning (9 AM–1 PM): outreach — Facebook groups, AI content, cold calls
- Afternoon (1 PM–5 PM): fulfillment — onboard new clients, weekly check-ins, implement services
- You: take the sales calls, close the deals
- Revenue needed to justify this: $3,000+/month

### Layer 2 — Add a Commission Closer

- Closer gets 50% of month-one revenue for each deal they close
- Example: Closer sells 2 clients at $500/month in a day → collects $1,000, keeps $500
- From month 2 onward: you keep 100% of the $500/month recurring
- You lose a little upfront. You build MRR without taking calls.
- A motivated closer can earn $15,000+/month — they have every incentive to perform.

### Layer 3 — Add Managers

- When you have 3 VAs and 3 closers, managing them directly consumes all your time
- Promote your best VA to VA Manager (manages all VAs)
- Promote your best closer to Sales Manager (manages all closers)
- You now manage 2 people instead of 6
- You are now at the top of a real pyramid

### Scaling Formula

| Stage | Monthly Cost | Monthly Revenue Target | Margin |
|-------|-------------|----------------------|--------|
| Solo | $0 | $3,000 | ~100% |
| VA added | $400 | $5,000–$8,000 | ~90% |
| VA + Closer | $400 + rev share | $10,000–$20,000 | ~70% |
| Full team | $2,000–$4,000 | $50,000+ | ~85%+ |

## 9. Ad Math Model

### The $25/Day Starting Model

| Variable | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Daily ad spend | $25 |
| Cost per booked call | $25 (target: 1 call/day) |
| Calls per day | 1 |
| Close rate | 25% |
| Clients per 4 days | 1 |
| Ad spend per 4 days | $100 |
| Revenue from 1 client | $500/month |
| **ROAS** | **5x** |

At $25/day: spend $100 in 4 days, make $500/month recurring.

### Scaling the Ad Model

| Daily Spend | Booked Calls/Day | Closes/Day (25%) | New MRR/Day | Monthly MRR Added |
|------------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------|-------------------|
| $25 | 1 | 0.25 | $125 | ~$3,750 |
| $100 | 4 | 1 | $500 | ~$15,000 |
| $300 | 12 | 3 | $1,500 | ~$45,000 |
| $1,000 | 40 | 10 | $5,000 | ~$150,000 |

**Rule:** Only scale ad spend after you've confirmed the system converts. Validate at $25/day first.

### When to Start Ads

- Start with organic (Facebook groups, cold calls) until you hit $3,000–$5,000/month
- Then add $25/day ads
- Once you confirm 1 booked call per $25 spent, scale to $100/day
- From $100/day: you're spending $100 to make $500/month recurring — infinite money machine in theory

### Ad Platform

- Facebook + Instagram (Meta Ads) — primary
- Target: business owners in your niche + geographic area
- Creative: short video ads (AI content via Arc Ads or HeyGen), CTA = "Call this number"

### 5. THE VIDEO + AUDIO INTERACTION UX

This does one thing: gets the appointment. The prospect shows up because there's zero risk. They're not committing to anything — they're just booking a call to learn more about a free trial.

#### On the Zoom Call — Closing Script

```
[After running the ROI calculator together and doing the

"So here's how this works. You have two options:

for your business and gives you price certainty.

OPTION 2 — Skip the Trial, Start Today:
If you already see the value and want to get moving, 
we can start today on a month-to-month basis. Same $500/mo, 
no contract, cancel anytime. You get the flexibility, 
we get started immediately.

Which one feels better to you?"

[Pause. Let them choose.]
```

#### The Psychology Behind This

1. **"Free trial" got them to the call.** Its job is done. It removed the barrier to booking.

2. **On the call, the question is NOT "do you want this?"** The ROI calculator and emotional close already answered that. The question is now: "Which payment structure do you prefer?"

3. **Option 1 (trial → 3-month contract)** sounds like the safer choice to the prospect, but it actually locks them in longer. The 3-month agreement gives you runway to prove ROI AND creates a commitment that's harder to cancel than month-to-month.

4. **Option 2 (month-to-month)** sounds like the flexible choice, but it gets them paying TODAY instead of 30 days from now. You skip a month of free service.

5. **Either way, you win.** Option 1 = longer commitment. Option 2 = faster revenue. The prospect feels like they chose. You never said "buy now or leave."

The fallback is always the free trial. It's the safety net that catches everyone who doesn't close on the first call. And once the system is live and generating leads for 30 days, they're not going to turn it off.

### 9. DYNAMIC LANDING PAGE TECH

The landing page needs to pull in the prospect's business name, type, and pre-filled calculator values from your outreach research. Here are the implementation options.

#### Option A — URL Query Parameters (Simplest)

```
yoursite.com/demo?biz=Johns+Plumbing&niche=plumber&city=Tampa
```

Page reads the params and personalizes:
- Headline: "Hey Johns Plumbing — here's what your after-hours calls would sound like with AI"
- Audio demo label: "Listen to Johns Plumbing's AI receptionist"
- Calculator pre-fills: Plumber LTV ($2,400), estimated missed calls for Tampa market
- Niche-specific copy throughout the page

**Pros:** Dead simple. No database needed. Generates instantly at outreach time. Zero backend complexity.
**Cons:** Long URLs look less clean. Params are visible (not a real problem for B2B). Limited data you can pass.

#### Option B — Dynamic Route with Database Lookup

```
yoursite.com/demo/j8k2m4
```

Short code maps to a prospect record in a database (or JSON file):

```json
{
  "code": "j8k2m4",
  "business_name": "Johns Plumbing",
  "niche": "plumber",
  "city": "Tampa",
  "owner_name": "John",
  "estimated_missed_calls": 25,
  "ltv": 2400,
  "audio_file": "johns-plumbing-demo.mp3"
}
```

**Pros:** Clean URLs. Can store more data per prospect. Can track page visits per prospect. Can pre-generate the audio file and link it.
**Cons:** Requires a database or data store. More setup. Adds a dependency.

#### Option C — Astro with SSR

Astro framework with server-side rendering. Dynamic routes render personalized pages at request time:

```
/demo/[code].astro → reads prospect data → renders personalized page
```

**Pros:** Fast, SEO-friendly (if that matters), modern framework, good developer experience.
**Cons:** Overkill for v1. Astro SSR adds hosting complexity.

#### >>> RECOMMENDATION for v1: Option A (URL Query Parameters)

Here's the full implementation spec:

```
URL format:
yoursite.com/demo?biz=Johns+Plumbing&niche=plumber&city=Tampa

Page reads params on load:
  const params = new URLSearchParams(window.location.search)
  const bizName = params.get('biz') || 'Your Business'
  const niche   = params.get('niche') || 'business'
  const city    = params.get('city') || ''

Page personalizes:
  Headline:    "Hey {bizName} — hear what your AI receptionist sounds like"
  Audio label: "▶ Listen to {bizName}'s after-hours demo"
  Calculator:  Pre-fills LTV based on {niche} from the reference table
  City:        "Serving {city}" badge (optional, adds local credibility)

Fallback (no params):
  Generic version — "Hear what YOUR business sounds like with AI"
  Calculator shows all niche options as a dropdown instead of pre-fill
```

Generate the URL at outreach time when composing the contact form message. Your outreach system already has the business name, niche, and city from research — just URL-encode them into the link.

Move to Option B when you need: per-prospect analytics (who visited, who listened, who used the calculator), pre-generated audio files linked to prospect records, or A/B testing different page variants per niche.
