# Quick Win Demos

> **Purpose:** Impress a prospect in 5-10 minutes during or after a discovery call.
> **Goal:** Get them to say "stop, I get it, how do I get this?"
> **Rule:** Every demo must produce something THEY can immediately use. Not theoretical. Not "look what AI can do." It's "here's something for YOUR business, right now, that you'd normally spend hours on."

---

## Demo Menu (Pick Based on Prospect Type)

### Demo 1: "Found Money" — The Instant Opportunity Audit

**Best for:** Revenue-focused owners, skeptics
**Time:** 5-7 minutes
**What you need:** Just their website URL

**What it does:**
Takes their URL and produces a "money on the table" report showing:
- 3-5 keywords they're ranking position 11-20 for (page 2 = invisible, page 1 = traffic)
- 2-3 pages with weak/missing meta tags losing clicks
- Quick-fix schema markup opportunities for rich results
- One competitor beating them that shouldn't be

**The script:**
> "Give me your website URL. That's all I need."
> [Run for 3-4 minutes]
> "Here's what I found. You're ranking on page 2 for [keyword] - that means Google already thinks you're relevant but you're invisible. With a few tweaks to this page [show specific page], you'd likely jump to page 1. That's probably [X] visits per month you're leaving on the table. And here - your competitor [name] is outranking you for [keyword] even though their content is thinner. Here's exactly why and what to fix."

**Why it works:** You just showed them money they're losing. That's not theoretical - it's specific to THEIR business, THEIR keywords, THEIR competitors.

**How to run it:**

```
Analyze [URL] and produce a "Found Money" report:

1. Find 3-5 keywords they likely rank position 11-20 for based on their content topics and page optimization. Look at their title tags, H1s, content depth, and topic coverage to identify terms where they're close but not page 1.

2. Identify 2-3 pages with weak or missing meta tags (titles too long/short, descriptions missing, no clear keyword targeting).

3. Identify schema markup opportunities (FAQ, HowTo, LocalBusiness, Product) that could earn rich results.

4. Find one direct competitor (similar business, same city/niche) that appears to be outranking them and explain why.

Format as a clean, scannable report with:
- A "Total Opportunity" summary at the top
- Specific pages and specific fixes (not generic advice)
- One clear "if you did just THIS ONE THING" recommendation
```

### Demo 2: "2-Hour Task in 2 Minutes" — The Live Content Brief

**Best for:** Time-strapped agency owners, content teams
**Time:** 3-5 minutes
**What you need:** One keyword they're targeting

**What it does:**
Takes a single keyword and produces a full content brief (the kind that normally takes 1-2 hours to research and write):
- Search intent analysis
- Recommended word count and content type
- Full H2/H3 outline
- Semantic terms to include
- Competitor content analysis
- Internal linking recommendations

**The script:**
> "What's one keyword you're trying to rank for right now? Or a keyword for one of your clients?"
> [They give you a keyword]
> "Watch this."
> [Run for 2-3 minutes]
> "Here's a complete content brief. Normally this takes your team what, an hour? Maybe two? I just did it in 2 minutes. And it's actually good - look, it analyzed what's ranking, found the gaps, structured the outline. Your writer could start on this right now."

**Why it works:** They just watched you do a task they do every week in a fraction of the time. The mental math is instant - "if this saves us 2 hours per brief and we do 10 briefs a month..."

**How to run it:**

```
Create a full content brief for the keyword: [KEYWORD]

Include:
1. Search intent classification
2. Recommended content type and word count
3. Full H2/H3 outline with key points per section
4. 15-20 semantic/LSI terms to include naturally
5. Top 3 competing content pieces and what they cover
6. What's MISSING from competing content (our angle)
7. Internal linking suggestions (generic - "link to your [topic] page")
8. Meta title and description recommendations

Format it professionally - this should look like a deliverable they could hand to a writer immediately.
```

### Demo 3: "Tool Killer" — The $150/mo Report in 30 Seconds

**Best for:** Cost-conscious owners, agencies paying for multiple tools
**Time:** 5-8 minutes
**What you need:** Their website URL + one month of basic data (or just the URL for a simulated version)

**What it does:**
Produces a client-ready monthly SEO report that looks like what AgencyAnalytics or DashThis produces - but without the $150/mo subscription:
- Traffic summary with trends
- Top performing pages
- Keyword ranking changes
- Technical health snapshot
- Recommendations for next month
- Professional formatting

**The script:**
> "What tool do you use for client reporting right now?"
> [They say AgencyAnalytics, DashThis, or manual spreadsheets]
> "How much does that cost you?"
> [They say $99-$199/mo]
> "And how long does it take to actually write the narrative and recommendations for each client?"
> [They say 1-3 hours per client]
> "Watch this. Give me your URL."
> [Run for 4-5 minutes]
> "Here's a report that looks like what your tool generates - but I also wrote the narrative, the recommendations, and the next-month plan. That's the part your tool CAN'T do that eats all your time. Now imagine this running for all 10 of your clients in an afternoon instead of a week."

**Why it works:** You just showed them you can replace a paid tool AND eliminate the manual work the tool couldn't do anyway. Double hit.

**How to run it:**

```
Create a professional monthly SEO report for: [URL]

Since we don't have their actual analytics data, create the TEMPLATE + STRUCTURE showing:
1. Executive summary section (with placeholder metrics formatted professionally)
2. Traffic overview section with a markdown table
3. Top 10 pages section
4. Keyword rankings section (use their actual visible keywords from their site content)
5. Technical health section (do a real quick audit of visible issues)
6. Recommendations section (these should be REAL based on what you can see)
7. Next month plan

Make it look like a real deliverable. Format headers, use tables, include specific observations.
The goal is for them to look at this and say "this is better than what my $150/mo tool gives me."
```

### Demo 4: "I Know You" — The Deep Business Intelligence

**Best for:** Skeptics, people who think AI is generic/useless
**Time:** 7-10 minutes
**What you need:** Just their website URL + maybe their Google Business listing

**What it does:**
Produces a comprehensive analysis that shows you understand their business at a level that would normally require hours of research:
- Their service offerings mapped out
- Their target audience identified
- Their positioning vs. competitors
- Their content strategy (or lack of one) assessed
- Their local market opportunity sized
- Specific, actionable recommendations

**The script:**
> "I bet you've had agencies pitch you before with generic advice that could apply to anyone. Let me show you something different. Give me your website URL."
> [Run for 5-7 minutes]
> "Okay, so here's what I see. You offer [services], your primary audience is [audience], you're positioned as [positioning]. Your top competitor in [city] is [competitor] and they're beating you because [reason]. You've got [X] blog posts but they're not targeting the right keywords for your actual services. And your Google Business Profile is [assessment]. Here are the 3 things I'd fix first if I were running your SEO..."

**Why it works:** You just demonstrated depth of understanding that normally takes a consultant days. They feel SEEN. And the recommendations are specific, not generic. This builds trust instantly.

**How to run it:**

```
Perform a deep business intelligence analysis of: [URL]

Analyze everything visible:
1. What services/products do they offer? (map their full offering)
2. Who is their target customer? (based on their messaging, location, pricing signals)
3. How are they positioned? (premium vs budget, local vs national, specialist vs generalist)
4. What's their content strategy? (blog frequency, topics, quality assessment)
5. What are their SEO strengths? (what they're doing right)
6. What are their SEO weaknesses? (specific problems, not generic)
7. Who are their top 2-3 competitors? (find similar businesses in their area/niche)
8. What's the #1 thing holding them back?
9. Top 3 specific recommendations with expected impact

Be SPECIFIC. No generic advice. Everything should reference THEIR actual pages, THEIR actual content, THEIR actual market. Make them feel like you spent a day researching their business.
```

**The script:**

> [Run specific skill for 2-3 minutes]
> "Same output. $0/month. And honestly this is better because [specific advantage - customized to their brand voice, more detailed recommendations, etc.]"

## Demo Selection Guide

During discovery, listen for these signals to pick your demo:

| They say... | Run this demo |
|-------------|---------------|
| "We're not ranking for anything" | Demo 1: Found Money |
| "Our team spends too much time on [task]" | Demo 2: 2-Hour Task |
| "We're paying too much for tools" | Demo 3: Tool Killer or Demo 5: Replacement |
| "I've tried AI before and it was useless/generic" | Demo 4: I Know You |
| "I don't see how this is different from ChatGPT" | Demo 2 or Demo 4 (action + depth) |
| "How does this actually work?" | Demo 1 (simplest, fastest, most impressive) |

## Rules for ALL Demos

1. **NEVER show them the terminal/code.** Share the OUTPUT only (screen share the markdown, not the process).
2. **Always use THEIR data.** Generic demos don't close deals. Use their URL, their keywords, their competitors.
3. **Have a "save" ready.** If something takes too long or errors, pivot: "Let me show you one that's already done for a similar business."
4. **End with the math.** After every demo, translate to hours or dollars: "That was 2 minutes. Your team normally takes 2 hours. At $50/hr, that's $100 saved per brief. You do 10/month. That's $1,000/month in labor alone."
5. **Don't oversell.** If they look impressed, STOP. Don't keep demoing. Ask: "So how many of these [briefs/reports/audits] does your team do per month?"
