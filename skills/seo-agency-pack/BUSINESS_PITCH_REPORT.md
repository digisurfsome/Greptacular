## The 5 Core Concepts He Teaches (Plain English)

| Concept | What It Is | Business Translation |
|---------|-----------|---------------------|
| **Prompts** | What you type to tell Claude what to do | "Describe what you want, attach screenshots, paste URLs - better input = better output" |
| **Claude.md** | Instruction manual Claude reads every session | "Write your brand rules once, benefit forever. Like a project brief for a permanent employee" |
| **Skills** | Specialist playbooks for specific tasks | "Turn Claude from a generalist into an expert at YOUR specific process" |
| **MCP Servers** | Connections to external tools (Notion, CRM, email, etc.) | "USB-C for AI - one standard connector to all your business tools" |
| **Context Window** | Claude's short-term memory (has a limit) | "One task per conversation. Fresh and condensed. Don't overload it" |

## His Qualifying Framework (Who This Is For)

He filters prospects through 4 questions:

1. **Are you already using AI?** (If just casual ChatGPT, not ready)
2. **Do you do the same tasks repeatedly?** (Content, reports, emails, data processing)
3. **Are those tasks costing you real hours?** (5-15+ hrs/week on pattern-based work)
4. **Are you willing to invest a few days learning?** (Not a magic button)

If yes to all 4 → Claude Code is right for them.

## His Key Framing Devices

### "Advice to Action"
- ChatGPT = texting a smart friend for advice
- Claude Code = hiring a smart assistant who sits at your desk and DOES the work

### "Fresh and Condensed" (Context Management)
- AI context is like milk - best served fresh and condensed
- One task per conversation
- Keep files lean, point to references
- This is the #1 reason people think it doesn't work (they overload it)

### "Generalist to Specialist" (Skills)
- Without skills = jack of all trades, okay results
- With skills = specialist hired for that exact role, following proven process every time

### "USB-C for AI" (MCP)
- One standard way to connect to any tool
- Don't connect 20 things at once - be deliberate
- Claude handles most of the technical setup

## Presenting to a Business: The Framework

### The Discovery Call Questions

Based on his qualifying framework + our skill set, here's the assessment process:

**Phase 1: Qualify**
1. "What AI tools are you currently using?" (If none, they're not ready)
2. "Walk me through a typical week - what tasks eat the most time?"
3. "What software/tools are you paying for right now?" (Find the replaceable ones)

**Phase 2: Identify Opportunities**
4. "What do you wish you could do but haven't found the right tool for?"
5. "What tasks do you do the same way every single time?" (These become skills)
6. "If you could get 5 hours back per week, what would you do with them?"

**Phase 3: Show Value**
7. Map their answers to specific skills from our pack
8. Calculate their current tool costs vs. what we replace
9. Calculate hours saved per week/month

### The Three Buckets (What Every Business Has)

| Bucket | Question to Ask | What We Do |
|--------|----------------|-----------|
| **Replace** | "What tools are you paying for that do ONE thing?" | Show them the skill that replaces it |
| **Improve** | "What are you doing manually that follows a pattern?" | Build a skill that does it 10x faster |
| **Enable** | "What have you always wanted but never built?" | Show them it's now possible in days, not months |

## Key Insight: Why His Dashboard Matters for This Pitch

The dashboard solves the #1 objection: **"This sounds complicated / I don't want to live in a terminal"**

With the dashboard:
- They never see the terminal
- They see their goals, not code
- They can drag tasks, see outputs, review in markdown preview
- It LOOKS like a business tool, not a developer tool

Without the dashboard, you're asking a business owner to type commands in a terminal. With it, you're giving them a project management interface that happens to have AI doing the work.
